Three things, done properly.

We are not building an observability platform. We are building the part that wakes you up.

Checks that mean something

Every 30 seconds from Mumbai, Frankfurt and Oregon. A check fails only when two of the three agree it failed, so a bad route in one region does not page you at 3am.

Alerts that reach a human

SMS first, then a phone call if nobody acknowledges within five minutes. Email is a courtesy copy, not the alert.

A status page you can point at

One public page per project, on your own domain, updated automatically when a check fails. No copy-paste incident updates.

Questions people have asked.

When does Beacon launch?

We are aiming for the first half of 2027. The waitlist is how we decide who gets in first and roughly how many checks we need to be ready for.

Do I need to install anything?

No. Beacon calls your public endpoints from the outside, the same way your customers do. There is no agent, no sidecar and no code change.

What counts as a check?

One URL, checked on one interval. A homepage and an API health route are two checks.

Will you sell my email address?

No. It goes in one file on one server and it is used to send you one launch email. Reply to that email with the word "remove" and it is deleted.

Who is building this?

Two engineers who have carried a pager for a combined fourteen years and have opinions about being woken up unnecessarily.
